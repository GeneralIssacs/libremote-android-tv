# Privacy policy

Libremote for Android TV ("Libremote", the phone app) and Libremote Receiver (the TV app) collect no personal data.
They have no accounts, analytics, advertising, crash reporting or servers.

## What the apps send, and where

- **To your TVs, on your local network:** the buttons you press, the text you type, the files you send, and the
  requests Libremote needs (opening an app, the TV's app list). Libremote does not tell the TV's remote service your
  phone's model or Android version.
- **Between Libremote and Libremote Receiver:** your phone's name, so the receiver can list it among paired phones,
  and a key that identifies the phone. Everything between them is encrypted, and the receiver accepts only phones
  paired with the TV.
- **To GitHub, only for updates:** when you check for updates, or turn on automatic checks, the phone app asks
  GitHub for the latest release and downloads it if you choose to update. GitHub sees your IP address, as for any
  download; see GitHub's privacy statement. Copies installed from an app store never do this.

Nothing else leaves your phone or your TV.

## What the apps keep

- **On the phone:** your TVs (name, address, the TV's key), a pairing key encrypted with Android's keystore, your
  shortcuts and settings, and the TV's app list and icons if you fetch them.
- **On the TV:** the paired phones (name, key, date paired), your receiver settings, and the files you send, in
  Download/Libremote.

Uninstalling an app deletes what it keeps, except the files you sent to the TV, which stay in Download/Libremote.

## Permissions

- **Phone:** network access (to reach your TVs, and GitHub for updates); notifications (progress of files you
  send; optional); a foreground service (to keep sending while you use other apps); installing apps (updates, in
  copies not installed from an app store).
- **TV:** network access; installing apps (the APKs you send, when you choose to install them); storage, on
  Android TV 8 and 9 only (to save files into Download/Libremote).
